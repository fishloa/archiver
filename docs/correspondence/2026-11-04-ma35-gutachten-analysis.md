## A. Where the case stands after 22.10.2026

**Accepted by MA 35:**
- Humprecht and Rudolf were persecuted ("unbestritten", 27.05).
- Felix was in Gestapo custody for Hoch- und Landesverrat from 28.03 to 07.04.1945, and was officially recognised as a victim (22.10).
- The regime persecuted close relatives of political opponents even where they had no involvement of their own (§ 83(2) and § 139 RStGB; the Haas, Cupal and Coppi cases) (22.10).
- It was "grundsätzlich möglich" that Alexander had grounds to fear persecution because of his brother (22.10).

**Two things remain against us:**
1. "Nicht sehr wahrscheinlich", because he was "in der Zeit der Verfolgung seines Bruders als Ingenieur an einem kriegswichtigen Forschungsprojekt beteiligt". This is a question of historical fact.
2. He left for a job at Fairey, so even well-founded fear was "mit Sicherheit nicht" the reason. This is mainly a legal question.

Everything else in the earlier reports has been conceded or overtaken.

## D. Overall argument (draft for counsel)

1. **The mechanism is conceded.** MA 35's historian accepts that the regime persecuted close relatives of people it pursued for high treason or resistance, even without their own involvement (22.10).
2. **Alexander was such a relative, three times over:**
   - **His brother Felix:** Gestapo custody for "Hochverrat und Widerstandstätigkeit". The charges were legitimism, defeatism, and the Falkenhausen circle with a named successor to Hitler, i.e. the milieu of 20 July, after which the regime applied Sippenhaft as policy. Felix was in the O5 resistance from 1943 and is a recognised victim (F2, H1–H2, B12).
   - **His first cousins Humprecht and Rudolf:** persecuted, one to his death, on pretextual charges, and over the family's property. Alexander was close to them by his own account, and his brother visited Humprecht in prison in 1943 (A1–A3, B0–B4, C1).
   - **His family:** in Heydrich's words, the danger lay in the nobles' connections "in die Ostmark", and the declaration was only the "Anlaß" (F1).
3. **The only counter-argument is an unproven premise.**
   - No document places Alexander on war work after 31.12.1943.
   - MA 35's own historian once called the only source unverifiable, then turned "dürfte" into fact without new evidence.
   - The only contemporary trace (30.04.1945) hopes he is at home in Aussee (B9).
4. **Even if the premise were true, it would not protect him.** Felix, a serving soldier, was arrested anyway (B10).
5. **The earlier grounds have fallen away:** kinship (proved), closeness (in Alexander's own words, in MA 35's file), Bohemian ties (his own words and the records), the Gauakt (closed before the danger, and largely a record of ignorance), and Felix (accepted).
6. **Procedure:** the Akteneinsicht request went unanswered until 3.11, and the Czech evidence was not assessed (C).
7. **Departure** is for counsel: whether a departure in 1946/47 satisfies the § 58c causal link ("weil"). The historian's "mit Sicherheit" rests on no date and no document (B13).
