- **1930–35:** his father seeks Czechoslovak citizenship with domicile at Dymokury (3965, 3966). ⚠ Use only the fact of the application, never the 1931 ruling.

- **1938:** his father dies at Schönhof and is buried at Dymokury (4040). His last visit to Dymokury was "some twenty five years" before 1963 (4019 p. 30).
- **1975:** his Austrian citizenship record is checked under the ČSSR Compensation Act (2023 report).

### 2c. Closeness to the cousins

- "Uncle Theobald lived in Dymokur … we saw his family quite often … I think we felt closer to them than to our other cousins" (4019 p. 23).
- The family were godparents to each other: Paul for Humprecht in 1909, and the grandmother for Rudolf in 1904 (4079, 4078).
- **During the persecution:** Alexander's brother Wolfgang visited Humprecht in Brandenburg-Görden on 11.04.1943 and applied to visit again in September (Arolsen 12120657 pp. 32–33, held; B4: the visit was arranged through "a relative who was a Reich German").
- **Alexander's own account** of the persecution: "German 'Zwangsverwalter' … took over the running of the estates … on trumped up charges, both were put into prison. Humprecht did not survive his ordeal" (4019 pp. 25–26). He wrote this decades before the StB file confirming the planted pistol became known.
- **After the war,** across the Iron Curtain: in 1963 he visited Rudobald in Prague ("we saw a lot of my cousins"), went with him to Dymokury, and visited his father's grave (4019 pp. 27–30).

**Distance:** Wohllebengasse 9 to Metternichgasse 10 is about **600 m in a straight line** (OpenStreetMap coordinates). The two addresses lie either side of the Belvedere and Schwarzenbergplatz area, in adjoining districts IV and III. Wohllebengasse 9 to no. 11 is about 30 m.

### The argument this supports

1. **Both brothers were in Vienna throughout Felix's O5 period.**
   - From July 1943, when Felix's O5 activity and the August 1943 "defeatist statements" begin, the two were registered about 600 m apart.
   - That continued until Felix's arrest in March 1945.
   - Alexander had himself lived at Wohllebengasse 11 in early 1943, next door to the building Felix moved into.
2. **Why this matters legally:**
   - MA 35's own historian (22.10, quoting Lojowsky) sets out the two provisions:
     - **§ 139 RStGB**: "Hierbei war es strafbar, wenn eine Person über staatsfeindliche Aktionen Bescheid wusste und dies nicht an die Behörden meldete" (up to life in Zuchthaus).
     - **§ 83(2) RStGB**: meant to allow "selbst die entferntesten Vorbereitungshandlungen zum Hochverrat bestrafen zu können, wobei hierbei auch jene gemeint waren, die lediglich mittelbar dienten". And: "Zahlreiche Personen wurden angeklagt, obwohl kein Hinweis vorlag aus dem hervorging, dass sie irgendeiner Gruppe oder Organisation angehörten."
   - On relatives, his own words: "Es gibt zahlreiche bekannte Fälle, bei denen im Zuge der Verhaftung von Widerstandskämpfern auch enge Verwandte verhaftet wurden, die mit Sicherheit nicht an deren Widerstandstätigkeiten beteiligt gewesen sind."
   - His examples:
     - The Cupal siblings: "unterstellt", i.e. alleged, to have known of their brother's plans, and sentenced to death.
     - Hedwig Raasch: "überhaupt nichts zu tun" with her daughter's resistance, and arrested anyway.
   - So on the historian's own account, knowledge was imputed to close relatives, not proved. A brother living a few streets away fits that pattern.
3. **The Gestapo's approach** in 1945 shows it treated the case as a political high-treason matter on RSHA orders, not as an ordinary end-phase arrest:
   - It came looking for Felix the day before his arrest.
   - It interrogated him for five hours on contacts going back to 1943.
   - It refused a search of his flat.
4. **The case to put:** from 1943 Alexander lived within walking distance of a brother who was active in the Austrian resistance and was eventually arrested for high treason. He had grounds to fear that he would be drawn in as one of the "enge Verwandte … die mit Sicherheit nicht an deren Widerstandstätigkeiten beteiligt gewesen sind". The historian accepts that mechanism.

### Limits: state them before MA 35 does

- **Registration is not proof of presence.**
  - The TH Wien file has him seconded to the WNF at Wiener Neustadt in 1943, writing from "Wiener Neustadt, Wienerstr. 67" on 4.8.1943 (record 3505 p. 35).
  - Felix was away with the Wehrmacht from August 1944 to March 1945.
  - Felix's diary, covering 26.3 to 6.5.1945, never mentions Alexander's flat. On 30.4.1945 he hopes Alexander is in Aussee.
  - So the two were registered neighbours. Daily contact is not documented.
- **No document shows that Alexander knew of Felix's O5 role,** or that the Gestapo asked about him. The argument is reasonable fear, not actual targeting.
- **Felix's flat** was in the IV. Bezirk; his § 27 file was handled there (M.Nr. 3315/IV. Bez.). Alexander's was in the III. The districts adjoin.
- **The police enquiry of July 1950** (Polizeidirektion Abt. I to the BMI) still used Metternichgasse 10/4 for Alexander. That confirms the address but not his presence in 1944–45.
