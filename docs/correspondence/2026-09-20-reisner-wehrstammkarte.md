# Oberst Markus Reisner — what became of Alexander after October 1943

**Sent 20 September 2026, Zimbra 465107**, into the thread Hubertus Trauttenberg
opened (465060). To `markus.reisner@bmlv.gv.at`, cc Prof. Kerstin von Lingen
(Universität Wien) and Hubertus Trauttenberg.

## How it arose

Trauttenberg wrote to Reisner on 19 September asking for his help as a historian,
saying Alexander had been a **Wachsoldat** at an aircraft works in Wiener
Neustadt, deserted in the chaos after the bombing, walked to Altaussee and buried
his uniform there.

**The place is right and the role is not**, and the archive can prove it. The TH
Wien personnel file (record 3505) has the Rektor writing on 7 September 1943 to
*"Herrn Dipl.Ing. Alexander Czernin in Wr. Neustadt, Wienerstr. 67"*:

> "…ist der wiss. Assistent Dipl.Ing. Alexander Czernin mit **25.11.1942** unter
> Aufhebung seiner Beleihung als Techn. Kriegsverwaltung **zur Luftwaffe
> versetzt** worden… **Ab 7.1.1943 ist Czernin als Arbeitsurlauber zu den
> Wiener-Neustädter Flugzeugwerken kommandiert** und wird von dort vom genannten
> Zeitpunkt ab vergütet."

So he was at the aircraft works as a **Luftwaffe-assigned engineer**, which fits
his own signature of 22 February 1942 as *Truppen-Ingenieur* with the number
17 578, and fits the British collecting him at Braunschweig in January 1947.

**The letter does not correct Hubertus.** It states positively what Alexander was
and encloses seven pages of the personnel file, leaving the historians to draw
their own conclusion — the applicant's instruction, and the right one in front of
third parties.

## What it asks

Not a list of questions, and not for a pointer: Hubertus asked them to do the
work, so the letter asks the same — to establish which unit he belonged to, how
his service continued after the secondment, and how it ended. Costs undertaken.

It records that the Bundesarchiv Abt. PA request of 19 September is expected to
take several months, which is why their help is worth having.

## Enclosure

Seven pages from TH Wien Personalakt (record 3505, pages 30, 79, 81, 101, 105,
107, 111): the Luftwaffe transfer, the secondment to the works, the 22.2.1942
letter, and the personnel card.

## The gap this is aimed at

**October 1943 to January 1947.** Nothing in the archive covers it. The desertion,
the walk to Altaussee and the buried uniform are family recollection only and are
not asserted anywhere in the letter.
