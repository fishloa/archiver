A file has come in that changes what we can prove about Felix, and one line in it
bears on Alexander. Both documents are attached, German original and English
translation side by side, with the archive's page numbers.

**What the file is.** Felix Czernin's complete Vienna Magistrat file on his
application under § 27 Verbotsgesetz — M.Nr. 3315/IV. Bezirk, Staatskanzlei
Zl. 13.413-2 N/1945 — together with his 1947 application under § 4 (5) VG 1947.
36 pages. Supplied by a cousin, Hubertus Trauttenberg; the originals have now
been ordered from the Wiener Stadt- und Landesarchiv and the Archiv der Republik.

## 1. The arrest is attested by the Austrian resistance, on oath

**Wilhelm Prinz Thurn und Taxis**, signing as *Leiter der Geschäftsstelle im
Generalsekretariat O 5*, made two statements (pages 4 and 13):

> Vienna, 10 May 1945 — "His arrest by the Gestapo took place **on the ground of
> resistance work abroad**. He was in constant contact with my group."

> Vienna, 28 August 1945, *an Eides statt* — "Felix Czernin **has belonged to my
> resistance group since 1943**. Czernin was deployed by me in particular for the
> **procurement of intelligence from abroad**, a task he undertook with skill and
> valuable results. On my instruction he made contact **in the autumn of 1943 in
> Turkey** with the group of the Austrian resistance movement there, **which
> ultimately led to his being arrested by the Gestapo in Vienna at the beginning
> of 1945**."

An officer of the O 5 leadership swearing that the arrest was caused by
resistance work he himself directed. This is not family testimony.

## 2. The Gestapo's own charges, recorded as they were put

Felix's diary (attachment 2, page 1) lists what he was charged with at
interrogation:

> 1.) In Aug. 1943 (**before the Turkey trip**) to have made defeatist statements
> about the war situation. 2.) to have said that **Falkenhausen** should take
> H.'s place. 3.) Relations with the **Falkenhausen clique**. 4.) **Turkey —
> enemy contact!** 5.) **In any case an Austrian Legitimist.**

Point 5 is the one that matters for § 58c: persecution for Austrian legitimism,
stated by the Gestapo, recorded the same day. Point 4 corroborates Thurn und
Taxis independently — the diary and the affidavit were written months apart by
different men and agree on Turkey.

## 3. The Austrian State recorded him as a victim of Nazi terror

Page 3 carries two documents. The *Vereinigung ehemaliger österreichischer
Widerstandsbewegungen* wrote to the Zentralregistrierstelle der Opfer des
Naziterrors on **5 March 1946** asking that he be registered, since he was held
*"from 27.3.1945 until the liberation by the Russian army on charges of high
treason and treason against the country"*. He was registered under **Nr. 38.835
on 16 March 1946** (page 28). A state register entry, not a statement of belief.

Below it, an attestation signed **Fritz Janich**: arrested by the Gestapo on
**28 March 1945**, held in the **Rossauer Kaserne until 7 April**, when the Red
Army's advance caused the remaining prisoners to be released with orders to
report at once to the **Wilhelmskaserne** Alarm-Kompanie —

> "**Czernin did not follow that order**, but kept himself hidden until 10 April,
> on which day the Red Army marched into the I. District."

## 4. The charge in his own hand, on an Austrian official form

The Meldeblatt of **6 November 1945** (page 2), which he signed, records that he
was conscripted into the Wehrmacht from early 1944 to 20 March 1945 and that he

> "was arrested in Vienna on 27 March 1945 by the Gestapo for **high treason and
> undermining military morale** (Hochverrat und Wehrkraftzersetzung)".

*On the date:* this form says 27 March, the Janich attestation and the diary say
28 March. The diary resolves it — arrival in Vienna on the 26th, the Gestapo came
looking on the 27th, the arrest was made on the 28th.

## 5. The line about Alexander

The diary, 30 April 1945 (attachment 2, page 11):

> "**Lucki's birthday. I hope he is at Aussee with Mami.**"

Vienna had fallen on 13 April and Felix was at liberty. So this is not a hope
that his brother survived the fighting; it is a hope about **where he was**. On
his brother's birthday Felix did not know where Alexander was, and the outcome he
hoped for was that he had reached the family's refuge at Altaussee rather than
being wherever he was supposed to be. He hoped Alexander was safe, which is to
say he had reason to think he might not be.

**Alexander is the only sibling named in the diary.** Felix had three brothers
and two sisters. Across the whole diary — 26 March to 6 May — Wolfgang is never
mentioned, Jan is never mentioned, neither sister is mentioned. Only Lucki, and
only in this hope about his whereabouts. The diary is otherwise full of named
people: Lippes, Karl Trauttmansdorff, Franzi Thurn und Taxis, Peter Habig,
Hansi Dumreicher. The silence about the other four is not a silence about family.

**The counter-reading, which the authority's historian will reach for:** that
30 April was Alexander's birthday, which is why he appears in that day's entry at
all, and that in the last weeks of the war everyone worried about everyone. The
answer to it is what Felix actually wrote when prompted by the birthday — not a
greeting, but a hope about **where** his brother was; and that Vienna had fallen
on 13 April, so the danger he was hoping his brother had escaped was not the
fighting.

One comparison from the same diary, 20 April: *"Thought a great deal about
**Ferschnitz** with fervent wishes for God's protection."* Ferschnitz, Edla 18 in
the Melk district, is where Felix himself re-registered from in November 1945.
The diary shows him anxious about two places — Ferschnitz, and Aussee, where he
hoped Alexander was.

What the line establishes on its face is that the brothers' situations were
linked in the family's own contemporaneous understanding, and that Alexander was
not where the authorities had him.

## 6. What is in the file that does not help us

The same Meldeblatt of 6 November 1945 records **NSDAP membership from December
1938 to 1945**, party card number approximately 6,900,000, and NSFK membership
from early 1939 to early 1941 as *"NSKK-Mann (Flugzeuglehrer)"*, with no party
function.

Any submission that quotes the arrest from that page has to be able to answer the
membership on the same page. The answer is in the file itself:

- Thurn und Taxis (page 4): in his economic position in the Sudetengau he was
  **compelled** to become a Parteianwärter in December 1938, which "was entirely
  understandable to me".
- Felix's own account (pages 8-9): a banker with the Böhmische Escompte-Bank,
  automatically made a German citizen on 13 March 1938; his promotion blocked in
  July 1938 by Reichenberg industrialists who called him *"politisch
  untragbar"*; the Aussig branch then taken over by the **Dresdner Bank**, which
  increased the pressure; he signed the Ortsgruppe's circular **expecting to be
  refused**, because not signing "could have been construed as a hostile
  demonstration"; a provisional card in March 1939; thereafter a **Parteianwärter**,
  a candidate and not a full member, taking no part in party activity.
- **The Austrian authorities accepted this at the time.** The Begutachtungs-
  kommission recommended in his favour on **5 December 1945** (page 19) and the
  Magistrat as Landeshauptmannschaft decided in his favour on **5 January 1946**
  (pages 21 and 30).

It is far better that we found this than that the authority's historian does.

## 7. One correction to something we have said

An earlier letter of ours (to the DÖW, February 2026) stated that the Gestapo
charges included "connections with Turkey **via Lucki**". **The words "via Lucki"
appear nowhere** — not in the diary, not in the § 27 file. The Turkey charge is
real and is in the diary; the attribution to Alexander was our own and is not
evidenced. It must not appear in any submission.
