# DRAFT — Jan Drocár: confirming which Czernins signed in September 1939

**Status: DRAFT, NOT SENT.** Held for approval.

**To:** Jan Drocár (supplier of records 3512, 3515, 3516, 3517)
**Subject:** Identifikace čtyř podpisů Czerninů na deklaraci ze září 1939

## Why we are asking

Mr Drocár supplied both the photograph of the autograph signature page (record 3512, from the
Český Šternberk archive) and the typed text with the full signatory list (record 3517). Between
them they give four Czernin signatures — Rudolf, Edmund, Humprecht and Jan Dypold — plus a fifth
name signed by proxy, "Jan Pálffy jménem svým a za hr. Františka Josefa Czernina".

Matching those forenames against the genealogy puts all five inside the applicant's close family:

| as signed | identified as | relation to Alexander Czernin (b. 30.4.1913 Vienna) |
|---|---|---|
| Rudolf hr. Czernin | Rudolf Děpolt Maria Josef Ludvík "Rudobald", \*Dymokury 25.8.1904, †Wien 16.5.1984 | first cousin |
| Humprecht Czernin | Humprecht Ottokar Paul Theobald Rudolf …, \*Dymokury 9.2.1909, †Pleš u Prahy 19.9.1944 | first cousin |
| Jan Dypold Czernin | Jan Nepomuk Děpold Rudolf Ferdinand, Premonstratensian Páter Václav, \*Dymokury 26.7.1915, †Wien 27.9.1967 | first cousin |
| Edmund hr. Czernin | Edmund Diepold Douglas Friedrich Ferdinand, \*Sofia 31.5.1907, †Salzburg 13.10.1994 | first cousin |
| za hr. Františka Josefa Czernina | František Josef Theodor Ottokar Maria, \*Praha 24.5.1878, †London 17.10.1963 | first cousin once removed |

Rudolf, Humprecht and Jan were brothers, sons of Děpolt Josef Ottokar Otto Maria of Dymokury.
Edmund was a son of Otto Rudolf Diepold Ottokar Maria. Alexander's father Pavel was their
brother. So the three fathers were brothers, and Alexander's grandfather Děpolt "Theobald" was
grandfather to all of them.

## The three questions

1. Are these the right men? The identification rests only on the forenames as signed, matched to
   the genealogy. Is Mr Drocár aware of any other Czernin of these forenames living in 1939 who
   could have signed instead — particularly another Edmund, or another Jan Děpold?
2. Does any surviving source record **František Josef Czernin** (1878–1963) as a signatory in his
   own right, given that he signed through Jan Pálffy? Is it known why he signed by proxy — was
   he already outside the country?
3. Is anything known of what happened to **Edmund** and to **Jan Děpold** during the occupation?
   The fates of Rudolf and Humprecht are documented (Hořejš in *Paměť a dějiny* 2014/04, and the
   Terezín Memorial's own prisoner record for Rudolf); nothing is yet known of the other two.

## What this is for

The §58c application turns on whether the persecution described in Heydrich's letter to Bormann
of 16 May 1942 — whose German original we hold from the Národní archiv, sign. 114-3-17, folio 66 —
reached the applicant's own family. If these identifications hold, two of the men who signed the
declaration that Heydrich gave as his reason were the applicant's grandfather's first cousins,
and both were tried and imprisoned. We do not want to put that to the Austrian authorities until
someone who knows the sources has checked it.
