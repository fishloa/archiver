# Archiv ČNB, and the Commerzbank referral

## What came back — Archiv ČNB, 18 September 2026 (Zimbra 465880)

Mgr. Lenka Vrchotová, č. j. **2026/159589/CNB/420**, answering the request of
16 September (Zimbra 463909, sent in Czech).

**The negative.** *"Osobní spis Felixe Czernina neevidujeme."* The fond Česká
eskomptní banka survives *"velmi mezerovitě"* — fragments of four branches only
(České Budějovice, Hradec Králové, Plzeň, Moravská Ostrava). Neither Hohenelbe
nor Aussig is among them.

**What she did beyond the question**, unasked, with four scans enclosed: looked
Felix up in the *Compass, finanzielles Jahrbuch*.

| Compass year | Entry |
|---|---|
| 1930 | Prokurist, Vrchlabí (Hohenelbe) branch |
| 1933 | head of that branch |
| 1939 | still head of that branch |
| 1940-1942 | **only** a member of the supervisory board of *E. Heuer, chemische Fabrik, Aussig* |

This is a third source for a sequence the log already holds twice — *Die Zeit*
announcing him as head of the Aussig branch in 1938 (record 3944), and his own
1947 statement of moving to the Continentale Bank in 1941. Corroboration, not a
new fact.

**Where the German banks' records went**, which is the durable part of the
letter: Ústřední likvidátor (1948) → Správa pro věci majetkové a devizové (1964)
→ Majetkový úřad (1993) → abolished 1998, rights to the **Ministerstvo financí**.
Recently transferred from the administrative archive to the **Národní archiv**,
with parts to **SOA Praha** and to the ČNB itself. Named contacts given: Mgr.
Martin Sovák and Mgr. Šárka Janotová (SOA Praha), `3na@nacr.cz` (Národní archiv).

And the referral that mattered: the Dresdner Bank bought the Escompte branches in
the annexed territory and the BEB became its affiliate, so the **Dresdner Bank
fond (1872-2002) is held by Commerzbank AG, Historisches Archiv**,
`geschichte@commerzbank.com`.

Thanked in Czech (Zimbra **465899**).

## The Commerzbank letter — sent 23 September 2026, Zimbra 465903

To `geschichte@commerzbank.com`, in German. Asks three things and nothing else:
whether a Personalakte or Personalkarte for **Felix Graf Czernin von Chudenic,
b. 7.3.1902 Hlušice**, survives in the Dresdner Bank holdings; whether the
holdings contain records of the Hohenelbe and Aussig branches or of the
Continentale Bank in Brussels naming him; and the conditions of use and cost of
reproductions.

Identification only — the branches and dates are what lets an archivist find a
man. **Deliberately absent:** any statement of purpose, and any mention of the
1947 interrogation of Felix by the **Dresdner Bank Trial Team**, which is where
most of what we know about these years comes from and is not something to put in
front of the bank's own historians.

**Expectations are low and deliberately so.** The applicant's instruction is that
further Felix banking material is not useful to the case; this went because it
costs one letter, not because the answer is needed. A private company archive
owes no reply.

## GStA PK's onward referrals — 23 September 2026 (Zimbra 465928)

Saskia Simons answered the thank-you with a referral list (Geschäftszeichen *zu*
653#00006#0331#0002). Four pointers, three of them the standard set:

1. **Personenstandsregister** are not at GStA PK; with the Standesämter since 1874.
2. **Naturalisations and police registration** were handled per *Regierungsbezirk*
   — look to the locally competent offices and the city or regional state
   archives; she suggests the **Landesarchiv Berlin**.
3. **Bundesverwaltungsamt, Köln** — responsible for evidence in citizenship
   determination and naturalisation proceedings, and holding records *"des
   ehemaligen Reichs- und Preußischen Ministeriums"*.
4. **Reichsstelle für das Auswanderungswesen** (earlier Reichswanderungsamt),
   whose fonds are at the **Bundesarchiv, Abt. Reich**.

**Not pursued, and the reason recorded so it is not reopened.** Only item 3 has
substance: the file we hold stops at the Reich clearance of August 1929 and never
says whether Wolfgang was in fact naturalised, and the BVA is where a successor
record of the outcome would sit. But the question is a **sibling's** citizenship
status. Alexander's own position is documented from his own papers — Austrian on
the 1936-1940 registration records, "D.R." on the Meldezettel of 9 October 1939,
and the Gauakt and Meldekarte for what followed. Three new correspondences to fix
a fact that does not move the application is not a good trade.

No reply sent: a second thank-you for a referral list is noise, and she has now
been thanked once for the substantive favour.
