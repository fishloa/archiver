Each application states the subject's death or presumed death (so section 40
cannot rest on a living person's data), the family's documented persecution, the
§ 58c purpose, and an offer to accept redaction of living third parties. Naming
§ 58c is safe here: TNA is a British archive and no part of the deciding state,
and a stated legal purpose is what makes an FOI review more than curiosity.

Four records found on 17 September 2026 carry closure status **D** (closed). Each would be
opened by a free "request a closed record to be opened" application under the Freedom of
Information Act, made through Discovery. All four are seventy to eighty years old and concern
people now dead, so the usual section 40 (personal data) objection is weak.

## 1. HO 405/9351 — CZERNIN, W, date of birth 10.09.1897 (1964–65)

The highest priority. On the evidence this is **Wilhelmine Czernin, née Clanner von
Engelshofen**, born Hoštakov 10 September 1894, died London 5 August 1974 — the widow of
**František Josef Czernin (1878–1963)**, who signed the declaration of the Czech and Moravian
nobility of September 1939 by proxy through Jan Pálffy. She naturalised on 9 June 1965
(HO 334/1115/85718, "alias Minne"). The file is her naturalisation case and should contain the
family's account of why they left Czechoslovakia and what happened to them there.

Grounds to state: the subject died in 1974; her husband died in 1963; their son Josef, born
1924, is the subject of the open file HO 405/9132; the applicant is a relative; the material is
sought for a citizenship application under §58c of the Austrian Citizenship Act in which the
persecution of the family is the issue.

## 2. HO 294/593/9655 — Anna Borek-Dohalska, b. 1911

Czechoslovak Refugee Trust case papers. Bořek-Dohalský is a declaration family in which the
persecution is already documented: **František Bořek-Dohalský** signed and was held in the Small
Fortress and then Dachau (prisoner 34390, 14 August 1942 – 29 April 1945), and his brothers
**Antonín** (Auschwitz, 3 September 1942) and **Zdeněk** (executed at Terezín, 7 February 1945)
both died. Anna's relationship to them is not yet established and the file should say.

## 3. HO 294/592/9583 — Robert Hildprandt, b. 1893

Czechoslovak Refugee Trust case papers. "Bratři Hildprandtovi" — the Hildprandt brothers —
signed the September 1939 declaration as a group, so a Hildprandt born in 1893 is very likely
one of the signatories themselves or their brother.

## 4. HO 405/33767 — LAZANSKY, O, born 30.10.1904

Naturalisation file of Otto Lažanský, who with Hedwig Lažanská has a Czechoslovak Refugee Trust
personal file (HO 294/281, 1939–1950) and naturalised in 1949 (HO 334/318/1891). Lažanský is not
on the signatory list read so far, so this is the weakest of the four and can be dropped if
applications are to be rationed.

## Not to be requested

**KV 2/3424 — Ferdinand Thun-Hohenstein** is already open and has been read. He was recruited
into the Abwehr in 1942 and worked against Italian resistance groups. Although Arnošt
Thun-Hohenstein signed the declaration, this file helps the other side of the argument and
should not be put in front of MA 35.

## Separately — a copy order, not an FOI request *(PARKED 18 September 2026)*

**FO 371/47178** (1945, open, not digitised) — "Request by Count Arthur Mensdorff-Pouilly for
assistance in releasing his family from imprisonment", Foreign Office Political: Northern:
Czechoslovak, Code 12 File 9565. Two Mensdorff-Pouillys signed the September 1939 declaration,
Alfons Karel and Edmund. **Caution:** the file sits in the Czechoslovak political series and is
dated 1945, so the imprisonment may be post-war internment by Czechoslovak authorities rather
than Gestapo imprisonment. That distinction decides whether the file helps the §58c case at all,
and it cannot be settled from the catalogue. It needs to be read before it is relied on — either
by ordering a copy from TNA's record copying service or by a researcher at Kew.
