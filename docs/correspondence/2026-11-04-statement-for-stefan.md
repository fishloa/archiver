## Part 1: summary of the case

**Where MA 35 now stands.** In its last opinion (22.10.2026) the historian accepts three things:
- The Nazi regime persecuted close relatives of political opponents who had no involvement of their own ("enge Verwandte …, die mit Sicherheit nicht an deren Widerstandstätigkeiten beteiligt gewesen sind").
- Alexander's brother Felix was held by the Gestapo for high treason and is a recognised victim.
- It was "grundsätzlich möglich" (possible in principle) that Alexander had grounds to fear persecution because of his brother.

Against this he puts a single reason: Alexander was supposedly working on a war-important research project at the time of Felix's persecution. The reason he left for Britain is treated separately (O17).

A reasonable person in Alexander's position had grounds to fear persecution, on two separate grounds.

> ### ★ The key document: Heydrich's own words
>
> Heydrich's 20th situation report to Bormann, Prague, 16 May 1942, marked "Geheime Reichssache" (Národní archiv 114-3-17, fol. 66; [P5](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/3796), filed 15.09.2026), under the heading "Enteignung des tschechischen Adels". This is the measure that fell on the Czernins of Dymokury.
>
> **1. The declaration of loyalty was only the occasion:**
> > "Als **Anlaß** wurde eine Treueerklärung genommen, die diese Adligen am 17.9.1938 … Benesch gegenüber abgegeben hatten."
> > (The occasion taken was a declaration of loyalty that these nobles had made to Beneš on 17.9.1938.)
>
> **2. The real reasons were the property and the families' links into Austria:**
> > "Es handelt sich um Grundbesitz …, der für die spätere deutsche Unterwanderung und Besiedlung von großem Wert sein wird. Gerade diese Adligen waren es, die … z.T. aufgrund ihrer deutschen Namen und **adliger Verbindungen ins Reich und in die Ostmark** sowie nach Ungarn nachrichtendienstliche, politisch sehr gefährliche Faktoren waren."
> > (This is landed property that will be of great value for later German infiltration and settlement. It was precisely these nobles who, partly because of their German names and their noble connections into the Reich and into the Ostmark, and to Hungary, were factors dangerous to intelligence and politically very dangerous.)
>
> **Why it matters:**
> - MA 35's whole case on the cousins (27.05, 12.08) is that they were persecuted *because of* the declaration, and so the persecution could not touch the Austrian branch.
> - The man who ordered the measure says the opposite. The declaration was the "Anlaß" (occasion). The reasons were the land and the nobles' family connections, named expressly as connections "in die Ostmark".
> - In 1942 the Czernins' connection in the Ostmark was Paul's family in Vienna: Alexander, his brothers Felix and Wolfgang, and their mother.
> - MA 35 has held this page since 15.09.2026. Its 24.09 opinion summarises the letter only as the "Zwangsverwaltung der Güter", and no opinion has ever addressed either sentence.
>
> ⚠ The letter does not order any measure against relatives in the Ostmark. The argument is that it gave the Austrian branch **objective grounds to fear**, which is the § 58c test, not that they were targeted.

### A. The Bohemian cousins: fear from 1942

1. **He was one of the family.**
   - Alexander's father Paul was born at Dymokury in Bohemia. He sought to recover Czechoslovak citizenship with domicile at Dymokury in 1930–35 (⚠ see Appendix C), and was buried at Dymokury in 1938 ([E4](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/4040)).
   - Alexander himself lived in Bohemia for much of 1919–38: at Hlušice 1922–27 and at Schönhof 1935–38 ([P1](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/4019); [E6](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/3504)).
2. **He was close to the cousins who were persecuted.**
   - Humprecht and Rudolf Czernin of Dymokury were his first cousins ([E1](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/4005)–[E3](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/4079)).
   - In his own words: "I think we felt closer to them than to our other cousins" ([P1](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/4019) p. [23](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/4019/pages/23)).
   - The families stood godparent to each other's children ([E2](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/4078), [E3](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/4079)).
3. **The cousins were persecuted, and not only for their declaration of loyalty to Czechoslovakia.**
   - Rudolf's estate was placed under compulsory administration in February 1942 and confiscated in April 1942. He was sentenced to three years and nine months.
   - Humprecht was arrested on 9.6.1942 when a pistol was "found" on his forester. On 30.6.1942 a Gestapo summary court sentenced him to death for possessing it; the hearing lasted at most 45 minutes. The sentence was commuted the same day to life in Brandenburg penitentiary. He was released dying and died on 19.9.1944 ([P6](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/3519); [E8](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/4008)). The 45 minutes are from Krýlová 2014, citing Kyncl 2012 (rec. [3518](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/3518); ⚠ check whether filed).
   - **Two independent sources, 30 years apart, give the same motive.**
     - Heydrich in 1942 values the confiscated land "für die spätere deutsche Unterwanderung und Besiedlung" (for later German infiltration and settlement) ([P5](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/3796)).
     - In 1972 Paul Feustel, the Kolín Gestapo chief who prosecuted Humprecht, testified that the Prague Gestapo chief Geschke wanted Humprecht before the summary court because of an "interest in seizing his property" ([E8](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/4008)).
   - **A Czechoslovak state investigation in the 1970s concluded that the pistol had been planted** to create the conditions for his "likvidace" (liquidation). The Regional Prosecutor in Prague indicted the forester on that basis on 30.8.1974 ([E8](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/4008)).
   - **Alexander's own words, written in January 1987:** "on trumped up charges, both were put into prison. Humprecht did not survive his ordeal" ([P1](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/4019) pp. [25–26](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/4019/pages/25)). This was 21 years before the state files were declassified (2008), from family knowledge alone.
   - **Even on the regime's own version, a Czernin cousin was sentenced to death for one pistol.** A member of the Austrian branch had every reason to fear what the regime would do to the family.
   - **Heydrich himself called the loyalty declaration only the "Anlaß" (occasion). He put the real reasons in the land and in the nobles' "Verbindungen ins Reich und in die Ostmark", which is where Alexander's branch lived ([P5](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/3796); see ★ above).**
4. **The persecution reached his immediate family.**
   - On 11.04.1943 his brother Wolfgang visited Humprecht in Brandenburg-Görden penitentiary. Wolfgang applied to visit again in September ([P4](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/4025)).
   - This was a wartime journey from Vienna to Brandenburg, to see a political prisoner whose file was marked "Tscheche!". It had to be arranged through a relative who was a Reich German ([E9](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/4009)).
   - The family's tie to the persecuted cousins was therefore active, visible and on the authorities' own records.

So, from 1942 at the latest, and certainly from the prison visit of April 1943, Alexander had grounds to fear that he would be treated as part of a family the regime had marked as hostile.

### B. His brother Felix: fear from 1943, and acute in 1944–45

1. **Felix was in the resistance.** He belonged to the O5 resistance group from 1943 and was sent to Turkey on its behalf in autumn 1943 ([P7](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/4061) p. [16](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/4061/pages/16)). The Gestapo later charged him with defeatist remarks dating from August 1943, links to the "Falkenhausen-Clique", "Feindverbindung" with Turkey and Austrian legitimism ([P3](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/3507)).
2. **The brothers were close.** On 30.04.1945 Felix wrote: "Luckis Geburtstag. Hoffentlich ist er in Aussee bei Mami" (Lucki's birthday; hopefully he is in Aussee with Mother) ([P3](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/3507) p. [11](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/3507/pages/11)). This was three weeks after his release from Gestapo custody, with Vienna just taken by the Red Army. Even then Felix remembered his brother's birthday, used his family nickname and worried for his safety.
3. **The brothers lived next to each other.**
   - From July 1943 Felix lived at Wien IV, Wohllebengasse 9/10 ([P7](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/4061) p. [1](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/4061/pages/1)).
   - Alexander had sublet a room at Wohllebengasse 11, the building next door, from January to February 1943. From 20.11.1943 he was registered at Wien III, Metternichgasse 10/4, about 600 m from Felix, and he stayed there until May 1946 ([E10](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/4021), E11).
4. **Alexander had no protected position after 1943.**
   - His secondment to the Wiener Neustädter Flugzeugwerke ran from 7.1.1943 and was extended to 31.12.1943. His university post was vacated on 1.10.1943 ([E13](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/3505)).
   - No document places him on any war project after 31.12.1943 (C10).
   - The regime had already refused to declare him indispensable ("UK-Stellung") in 1941 ([E13](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/3505)).
5. **Usefulness to the war effort protected no one.**
   - Felix was a serving soldier in 1944–45. He was arrested when he reported to the Wehrkreiskommando, and held "da ich Soldat sei" ([P3](https://archive.czernin.eu/records/3507)).
   - After 20 July 1944 the regime punished the relatives of suspected conspirators as a matter of policy. That included brothers who were themselves serving soldiers, such as Alexander von Stauffenberg (E14, E15).
6. **The fear is the very mechanism the historian accepts.**
   - § 139 RStGB punished anyone who knew of treasonable activity and did not report it.
   - The historian's own examples are close relatives presumed to know and arrested anyway: the Cupal siblings were "unterstellt", and Hedwig Raasch had "überhaupt nichts zu tun" with her daughter's resistance.
   - A brother registered a few streets away is exactly such a relative.

### C. The departure

He left Austria in January 1947, inside the § 58c window, which runs to 15 May 1955. The Vienna Administrative Court accepts departures after 1945 where persecution before 9.5.1945 caused them, the "nachwirkende Verfolgung" (persecution with continuing effect) line (E16). The job at Fairey was the means of leaving, not the reason. MA 35's own 2023 opinion attributed the move to "Bestrebungen der britischen Regierung" (the British government's initiative). The 2026 opinions admit they do not know when he left, yet state his motive "mit Sicherheit" (C15).

## Appendix C: for counsel only (not to be filed)

### C-1. Weak points, strongest objection first

1. **The departure motive (O17).** This is a legal question.
   - The Vienna court's test is that persecution must be "ausschlaggebend" (decisive), not "wirtschaftliche oder familiäre Gründe" (VGW-152/099/8601/2022, p. 14), and Fairey is an economic reason.
   - In that case the persecution was *suffered* (Mauthausen), and the motive was carried by a witness and an expert report. We have neither.
   - Questions for you:
     - Is there case law on "nachwirkende *befürchtete*" (feared) persecution, as opposed to persecution suffered?
     - Does a contributing motive suffice?
2. **The war project (O15).**
   - Vertipedia has the Doblhoff group moving to Obergrafendorf after a bombing raid (undated) and to Zell am See in spring 1945, without naming Alexander. It records the post-war decision that "Czernin and I would go to Fairey" being taken at Zell am See.
   - His Vienna registration from 20.11.1943 neither proves nor disproves that he worked at dispersed sites. Vienna-Floridsdorf, Schwechat and Hinterbrühl are all within commuting distance; Obergrafendorf, about 65 km away, is not.
   - Our case therefore rests on the absence of any document after 31.12.1943, and on MA 35 bearing the burden.
3. **No public stand by Alexander, and the NSDAP relatives (O5, O11).** These can be answered only by logic and by what happened to Felix.
